Effort vs result at a level

Does a level-anchored window — "since price arrived here, buyers have spent N contracts and gained M ticks" — say anything the chart's existing absorption band does not? The band already scores volume / max(range, tick) over fixed 15-second windows (replayEngine.ts:1305), so this page is the check that runs before the reading gets pixels, not after.

Q0 · How long is a contact the sentence assumes price stays put

A contact opens within __CONTACT_TICKS__ ticks of a level and closes when price leaves by __EXIT_TICKS__. Everything downstream rests on how long that lasts, because "N contracts spent" is only a number worth reading if N has time to accumulate.

Q1 · Is it already drawn overlap with the absorption band

An episode counts as covered when a published band intersects it in both time and price. The denominator matters as much as the answer: bands light only a sliver of the session, so a low overlap is what any episode set would score, including a randomly placed one.

Q2 · Can a fixed baseline score a variable window the normalisation question

Absorption's median is taken over windows of one fixed length. A since-contact window has no fixed length, so before it can be scored against anything, its concentration has to not depend on how long it ran. It does.

Q3 · Does it separate anything forward __FORWARD_MIN__ min, signed against the aggressor

Positive means the side doing the spending was wrong afterwards — what an absorption read claims to see coming. The flag is heavy one-sided effort (top tercile) that gained no ground.

The flag has two terms and only one of them is order flow: "gained no ground" is a displacement fact that needs no tape at all. Holding it fixed — taking only the episodes that lost ground — isolates what knowing the size spent actually adds.

A null is only worth reporting if the harness could have found something, so the same forward join is scored against terms that are not order flow at all. These are not proposals — they are the proof that a reading of 0.5 above means the signal is absent rather than the join broken.

Q4 · Sweep and reclaim the pattern the episode split cuts in half

The episode definition has an obvious hole, so it is measured rather than argued about. Price wicks through a level and comes back — the single most readable thing that happens at a level — and the hysteresis turns it into two episodes split at the excursion, with the reversal falling in the gap. Neither episode contains it. Scored on its own terms below: positive means the reclaim direction was right afterwards. The unfiltered set is dominated by two-second flickers, so the rows beneath it keep only excursions that would print a wick you could see.

Sweep neither threshold is pinned by anything

Both edges of the hysteresis are free parameters, so they are swept rather than defended. The last three rows hold the contact edge at 4 ticks and widen only the exit — the direct test of "let the wick stay inside one episode". It does fix the duration: at an 80-tick exit the median contact reaches 39s. But watch the range exponent, which stays flat at +0.08 across a ten-fold widening. The range is pinned by the threshold at every setting, so concentration never stops measuring the setting — and the tape's marginal contribution stays at a coin flip while the halves come apart (0.539 / 0.441 at the widest). Widening also costs something the table cannot show: at 80 ticks, "price is at the level" describes price twenty points away from it.
