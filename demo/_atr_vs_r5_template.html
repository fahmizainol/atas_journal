The ATR line vs the one number

Top pane: the session's candles. Bottom pane: the same volatility read two ways — the ATR(__ATR_PERIOD__) line exactly as an indicator draws it (in ticks), against three flat references: yesterday's median bar range (at the chart's bar size — the 5-minute version is the one causal number the vol-scaled sizing rule uses), today's median (known only in hindsight), and the current 50-tick stop. The wiggle of the line against the stillness of the flat ones is the whole comparison: the indicator hands you a range that drifts all session; the rule needs one number, fixed before the open. The bar-size switch redraws everything at that resolution — the rule's numbers stay 5-minute.

How to read it, and what each number is
  • The blue line is your indicator, verbatim — Wilder's ATR(__ATR_PERIOD__) on __BAR_MIN__-minute bars, warmed through the overnight so it has a value at 09:30, converted to ticks (×4). Its first hour is mostly the open-spike being digested; after that it still drifts by ~2× within a session. That drift is why "read the ATR" never produced one number you could size from.
  • The green dashed line is the number the sizing rule uses — yesterday's 10:00–16:00 median 5-min bar range. Yesterday's, because a rule keyed to today's full-session figure is lookahead: you can't know it at entry. Median, not mean, so the open and single monster bars don't drag it.
  • The rule: V = yesterday's median ÷ __R5_REF__t · stop = __STOP_BASE__t × V · size = floor($500 ÷ stop) MNQ, which caps risk at $__RISK_USD__. Target and trail scale by the same V. At V ≈ 1 it collapses to the current 1 NQ × 50t exactly.
  • Orange vs green gap = the rule's honest error: vol autocorrelates day to day but not perfectly, so yesterday's number misses today's by that gap. It is still far steadier than eyeballing the blue line.
  • The yellow line is today's number read live — the expanding median of bar ranges since 10:00, which by construction ends the day on the orange line. Where it flattens early (often by ~11:00), the day's character was knowable mid-morning; where it climbs all afternoon (a vol event arriving), no pre-open number — yesterday's included — would have caught it. It is drawn to show how early the day is knowable, not as an invitation to resize mid-session: unlike the goldfish-memory ATR it never un-learns the morning, but a rule that re-reads it after entry is still the feel-driven adjustment the study record argues against.
  • The 1-minute view is a trap if read alone. 1-min bars are individually about half the height of 5-min bars, so the same 50t stop "covers" twice as much of one — but the risk didn't change: the same move just arrives as several stacked candles. That's why the sizing rule stays keyed to the 5-minute number whichever chart is displayed. Flip the switch on the same day and watch the coverage percentages move while the tape doesn't.
