A terminal for split panes — and the trade panel that goes with it

The Charts workspace already draws no shell chrome, and it already splits — into exactly two panes, one tradable and one read-only. Two is where the current chrome stops working. Everything the page floats over "the chart" — the legend, the tool rail, the order dock, the timeframe control on the bar — was written when there was one chart to float over, and each answers which chart? in its own way or not at all. Going to arbitrary layouts forces the question, so this page answers it in one place: tools to a left rail, layout and bucketing to a top bar acting on the focused pane, what-is-drawn to a per-pane legend, and order entry to a right dock plus a ticket that floats over the trading pane. Candles are a real cached NQ session — chrome that survives a synthetic sine wave proves nothing.

Prototype, not a build. Nothing here is wired into the app and no gesture on this page can reach a broker. The account selector, the guardrail meters, the review sentence and the fills are all mock — driven by a random walk around the session's last price, so the numbers move and the states are reachable. Everything about the chrome is real: the layouts switch, the dividers drag, the tools arm and draw, the panes sync.

What moved, and why

Tools Rail floating inside the canvas, top-right. Belongs to one chart; a split gets two rails, or one rail that silently acts on the left chart. Left rail, outside every canvas. A tool is a mode of the terminal, and it acts on the focused pane. One rail however many charts there are — and it stops eating chart pixels.
Bucketing On the bar, meaning "the trading chart". The context pane grew its own picker bottom-left because the bar's could not reach it. On the bar, acting on the focused pane. One control, four charts, and the second picker goes away.
Legend One, top-left of the chart. One per pane — unchanged in kind, because what is drawn was always per chart. Collapses to the identity line on a pane under ~360px.

The five questions a grid forces

1 · Which chart does a control mean?

The answer is focus, and it only works if focus is impossible to mistake. Here it is a 1px accent ring on the pane plus the pane number spelled out on the bar beside the control that acts on it. Click anywhere on a pane to focus it; 1–4 jump. A quieter treatment was tried first and failed the obvious test: with two 15m panes side by side you could not tell which one the timeframe button was about to change.

2 · What is shared and what is not?

Shared: the session, the instrument, the account, the tool arming. Per pane: bucketing, indicators, scale, and the visible range unless linked. The rule of thumb is that anything you would answer with "the session" is shared and anything you would answer with "this chart" is not — which is also why linking is a toggle rather than a default. Two views of the same tape at different bucketings should scroll together; four charts you are using as four different questions should not.

2b · Link means the same right edge, not the same window

The obvious implementation — push one pane's visible range onto the others — is what TradingView does and it is wrong for mixed bucketings. The first build of this page did it, and the 2×2 was the proof: an hourly pane forced into a 5-minute pane's window shows four candles. So link here pins every pane to the same right edge and leaves each one its own span. Scroll back an hour anywhere and every pane moves back an hour, each still showing the amount of history its bucketing is for.

3 · Where does the trade surface go?

Not in a pane. Order entry is a property of the account, and there is exactly one account — putting a pad in each pane would draw four of a thing you have one of. It goes in a dock outside the grid. The ticket that duplicates its two most-used buttons sits on one pane and stays there until you move it — drag it anywhere, double-click its grip to bring it to the pane you are working in. The pills are the opposite case: they are the state of the account rendered at a price, so they belong on every pane at once.

4 · What does a fourth pane cost?

Measured, not guessed: the chart engine is cheap and linear — roughly 65% of a core per repainting pane, flat in pane size. So the cost is the repaint rate, not the pane count. Panes you are not working in update on new bar rather than on tick; the focused pane keeps the live update. Four panes on tick is the one configuration this design does not offer.

5 · What happens on a narrow window?

Below ~1100px the dock collapses to a button and the ticket carries the whole order surface. Below ~900px the grid falls back to the focused pane alone — the split is hidden, not unset, so the layout you chose on a desktop is still there when you come back to it. The mobile pass stops at the Lab and this does not change that.

Keys

1–4 focus a pane · L layouts · T trade panel · Esc disarm the tool · Space+click a price to place · q/w buy/sell · s flatten. The order keys are page-level and mean the same thing wherever the pointer is — a market order does not belong to a chart. Only the pointing gestures (space+click, ＋Order) are per pane, because those name a price and a price has to be read off something.

The order pills

A level on a chart has to say two things: what it is and what it is worth. The prototype now draws the app's own chips — BUY LMT ×1 · 12.50 pts on the resting price, SL −$200 and TP +$400 · 2.0R on the legs behind it, and once it fills, LONG ×1 +$120 ticking against the tape.

  • No prices in the pills. Every level already names its price in an axis label the colour of its own line. Printing it twice only makes the pill long enough to cover the candles it sits over.
  • Money, measured on the routed contract. The same 40 ticks is $200 of NQ and $20 of MNQ. Point the dock at the micro and every pill, meter and sentence on the page re-denominates — because the number you read has to be the number that gets booked.
  • A working order's legs are prospective. They are measured off the resting price, not a fill, and they are what the order would carry. Once there is a position, the bracket belongs to the position and the pills change hands — blue and amber for "not money yet", green and red for "money".
  • Lines stay true, pills get nudged. A 40-tick bracket is about 20px apart on an hourly pane, so the pills collide and the thing that loses is the ✕. The line always sits at its real price; only the pill slides, keeping top-to-bottom order.

What it costs to let every pane trade

Less than it sounds, and the reason is a decision made a long time ago: WorkingOrderView carries no x-coordinate. A working order is a horizontal line, so it needs a price and nothing about where it sits in the tape — which is why the same pill draws on a 1-minute pane and an hourly one with no per-pane work at all.

  • Pills in every pane: one line per page. The context pane is already handed the position line and the trade marks (chart2Ref.current?.setPosition(…), .setTrades(…)). It is never handed setOrders. Adding that call is the whole of it.
  • Placing from any pane: pass the props you already withhold. The callbacks are price-based and the chart component is already pane-agnostic. What pane 2 lacks is onPlaceOrder / onOrderMove / onOrderCancel / ticket / canPlaceOrders — the same values pane 1 gets.
  • The keyboard problem is already solved. lib/chartFocus.ts elects one pane as the keyboard owner on pointer-enter or press, precisely so Escape and Delete aimed at one pane do not act on the other.
  • The real work is page-level plumbing, not chart work. Two named refs become an array, and the six places that drive them (setTape, setSnapshot, applyStep, setPosition, setOrders, setTrades) become loops. prefsPane="a"|"b" becomes an index. One QuickDock for the page rather than one inside each pane.
  • And one engine per pane. The second pane already runs its own replayEngine at its own bucketing; four panes is four engines. That is the actual cost, and it is the one already measured — ~65% of a core per repainting pane, so the non-focused panes want to repaint on new bar rather than on tick.

What you give up is the sentence "there is no gesture on that pane that could reach the blotter", which is currently true by construction and would become true only because the guard layer says so. That is the part worth arguing about — not the wiring.

The trade panel: why two surfaces and not one

They answer different questions and get used at different tempos, and collapsing them costs one of the two.

  • The dock is the account. Which account this is (drawn in every state, including the ones where nothing can be sent), what the guardrails have left, what the broker says is working, and the one place you can type an order rather than point at one. It is read before a session and consulted during one. Its kind badge is the load-bearing pixel on the page: the failure this whole feature exists to prevent is a funded account that looked like a paper one.
  • The ticket is the trade. Two buttons, a size, a bracket, and the position — over the chart, under the thumb, draggable to wherever your layout is quiet. It carries no account state at all beyond being disabled when the dock says you cannot send.
  • Review lives in the dock only. An order you type gets a sentence and a single-use token before it goes; the ticket, the hotkeys and the chart gestures send immediately, because putting a confirm step in front of the button you press when price is moving is how you train yourself to dismiss confirms without reading them. One-click removes the sentence from the pad too, for the sessions where the pad is the fast path.
  • Flatten is in both, always enabled. The moment you want it most is the one where something is behaving in a way nobody planned.
  • The ticket does not follow focus, and that is not a convenience call. It did, in the first build of this page, and the bug it caused is the argument: clicking low on an unfocused pane re-parked the ticket at that pane's bottom-centre between mousedown and mouseup, so the ticket swallowed the click — and the next one would have landed on BUY. A surface carrying market-order buttons must never arrive under the pointer as a side effect of a gesture aimed at something else.

Open questions this prototype does not settle

  • Does the trading pane follow focus, or stay put? Here it stays put and moves only when you press its badge — deliberately sticky, because "the chart I trade" changing because I clicked another chart to read it is exactly the class of surprise that costs money. The opposite choice is defensible if you find yourself pressing the badge constantly.
  • Per-pane instruments. The grid is one session and one instrument throughout. NQ/ES/CL in different panes needs a symbol-link group model and a second tape subscription, and it is a bigger change than the chrome — worth deciding before the layout model is built, not after.
  • Saved layouts. Layout + per-pane bucketing + indicators is a workspace, and workspaces want names. Not prototyped; the shape it would take is the existing chartPrefs blob with a list around it.
  • Does the left rail earn 38px? On a 1080p window the rail plus the dock is 358px of 1920. The rail pays for itself the moment there are two panes (one rail instead of two, and no tools drawn over price); on a single pane it is a straight loss against today's in-canvas rail.
