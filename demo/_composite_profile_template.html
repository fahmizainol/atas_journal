Composite volume profiles

A composite merges several sessions into one distribution, and you read levels off the result. The unasked question is which sessions belong in it. Four rules, same data — watch the shape, not the levels. Stretch the window and the value area inflates until it stops naming a location: ~226 pt under the balance rule, ~1630 pt over 20 sessions. A value-area edge that wide is not a level.

This is a viewer, not a study. Nothing here is scored against forward returns. It shows what each compositing rule produces so the rule can be chosen on structure rather than convention. Our own VP-level work has repeatedly come back null — see the notes at the foot before treating any of these levels as tradeable.

How this is put together
  • Data — the existing tick cache only (data/cache/ticks/*.parquet), RTH. Nothing is fetched.
  • The balance rule — accumulate sessions while each new one's value area still touches the composite-so-far's; restart on a clean break (new value entirely above or below). Capped at __CAP__ sessions as a runaway guard. This is the orthodox Market Profile construction: the market sets the boundary, not a lookback constant.
  • HVN / LVN by prominence, not height — a hump counts only if it stands clear of the deeper valley flanking it by the prominence setting, measured against the tallest hump; smoothing is the rolling window as a share of the price range. Both are live controls, because the right setting is not knowable in advance — that is the thing to feel out. At 15% prominence almost everything is a node; at 60% only the dominant hump survives. Raw height would call every shoulder on the POC a second node. LVNs are read only between two accepted humps; a trough at the edge is just where the auction stopped.
  • What the width means. The headline on each card is the median 70% value-area width that rule produces. The point of a composite level is that it names a price; once "value" spans most of the range, VAH/VAL are describing the market's whole excursion rather than anywhere it agreed on.
  • The validity check is a split value area, not bimodality — and it does not discriminate between the rules here, because NQ sessions are frequently split on their own. It is shown because it is true of the data, not because it settles anything. Bimodality alone proves even less — a single-session double distribution is a textbook trend-day shape, and ~30% of lone NQ sessions print two prominent humps even at a strict threshold. What actually breaks a composite is a deep trough inside its own value area: flagged when the lowest point between VAL and VAH falls under __SPLIT__% of the tallest hump. Then VAH/VAL are still real boundaries, but the band between them is two pockets of acceptance with rejected prices in the middle, and its POC is not "the fair price of this auction".
  • The live view answers the usage question the static view can't: the composite is frozen at the prior close and today is measured against it. Scrub through the session in __LIVE_STEP__-minute steps — the composite profile grows right from the centre line, today's developing profile grows left, and the read-out names the relationship (accepting inside prior value, extending through VAH/VAL, building value clear of it). Letting today feed the composite would be circular: the POC would drift toward wherever price sat, so the level could never be meaningfully violated. Every checkpoint is strictly cumulative from the open, so nothing reports a level the session had not yet built.
  • Events are a stand-in, and a leaky one. Pulcini's step 2 is an event at the level — an MBO-labelled iceberg or stop-run, which is exactly the feed we don't have. Two things reachable from trade prints stand in for different halves of it. A sweep burst is aggressive size arriving at once: consecutive same-side fills glue into one order-shaped sweep, sweeps over __SWEEP_LOTS__ lots that land within __BURST_GAP__ s and __BURST_SPAN__ pt of each other glue into a burst, and a burst needs __BURST_LOTS__ lots to count — the stop-run half. Absorption is size trading with nowhere to go: a __ABSORB_WIN__-second window whose lots-per-point-traversed runs __ABSORB_MULT__× the session's own median — the iceberg half. Neither is the MBO label. A sweep is the aggressor's footprint, not the resting order that refilled; absorption cannot tell a refilling iceberg from a thick crowd of small passive orders. The threshold is relative to the session on purpose — a 4-point band means nothing on a day whose median 15-second range is 12 points, and an absolute one finds everything or nothing depending on the regime. The size switch is in units of each kind's own floor, so 2× means twice the burst minimum and twice the concentration multiple.
  • Why there is a null next to the overlay. Events land where price traded, and price traded most where the composite says value is — so "the events stack at the POC" is true of any subset of the tape, including a random one. The read-out therefore puts the events' distance-to-nearest-level beside the same distance for the session's own volume distribution. If the events aren't nearer than that, the picture is showing where the market was, not where it reacted. Distances use POC / VAH / VAL only, so the number doesn't move when the HVN/LVN knobs do. In the static view the comparison is circular — those sessions built the level — so it is only scored in the live view, where the composite was frozen before the events printed.
  • The price panel — bars laid end to end with session gaps removed, so the x-axis is sequence, not clock. The timeframe switch is cosmetic: profiles, value areas and nodes are all computed from raw ticks at 0.25-point resolution, never from candles. Each session's own value area is shaded behind it, which is what the balance rule is reading.
  • Prior results — HVN/LVN is the most-tested layer in this repo and it has not survived: structure-break × node was refuted (LVN breaks net worse; thin zones are symmetric volatility, not direction), LVN-retrace lost to a random-pullback null, VP levels don't hold better than fresh ones, and there is no POC magnet. The multi-session composite is the one VP cell that hasn't been null-checked — which is the reason to look at it, and the reason to null-check it before building anything.
