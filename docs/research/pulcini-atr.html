Pulcini's ATR skeleton

All nineteen of his named setups are the same five steps — only step 1 changes. Steps 3–5 are pure geometry in ATR units, which is the part we can draw from ticks alone. Pick an event in the table to lay the skeleton over the chart.

Not a backtest. There is no exit rule here, no trade management, and no sample worth a statistic. The page draws where the geometry puts the entry and the stop, and reports how far price then travelled — it does not claim the setup makes money. The volume event is a stand-in (see the notes at the foot).

How this is put together
  • Data — the existing tick cache only (data/cache/ticks/*.parquet). Nothing is fetched, so the page costs nothing to rebuild.
  • ATR — Wilder's ATR on 5-minute bars, read off the last bar to have closed before the event. The bar size is his — he says "5-minute ATR" outright. The lookback is not: the episode never names a period, so the switch offers 5, 10 and 14 (Wilder's default). Flipping it barely moves anything — 5-min NQ vol is persistent enough over 25–70 minutes that ATR(5) and ATR(14) land within a few points of each other. That is the useful result: the wide stop is not an artifact of a lookback we guessed at. The bar the event lands in is still forming while a trader would be reading the number, and its range is partly the event itself. Bars run through the overnight so the number is already warm at 09:30 — otherwise the first 70 minutes of RTH, where most events land, would have no ATR to scale by.
  • The event is a proxy, and a leaky one. His trigger is an MBO-labelled iceberg or stop run. From trade prints, a burst of size and a stop run look identical. What's drawn is: sweeps of ≥50 lots (consecutive fills merged into order-shaped units), clustered when they land within 60 s and 5 pt of each other, kept when the cluster totals ≥150 lots. The zone is the price span the burst printed across. Absorption-flavoured detectors have died in our own A/Bs more than once — this one is here to hang the geometry off, not because it is believed.
  • Confirmation window — price gets 60 minutes from the end of the burst to escape by ATR × multiplier, in either direction; whichever side goes first sets the side. Events that never escape are listed as no escape and are worth as much attention as the ones that do.
  • Reach / offside — the furthest price got onside and offside within 120 minutes of entry. There is no target and no exit, so these are distances, not results.
  • Sizing — $500 ÷ (stop distance × $2/pt) on micros, rounded down. A wide ATR stop with small size is the same dollar risk as a tight stop with big size; that is his entire argument.
  • The fixed-stop column — the identical entry carrying a 15-point stop instead. first means the fixed stop was touched before the ATR stop, i.e. the trade was ended by noise the ATR stop never registered. His "risking 15 NQ points in a 180-ATR regime" line, measured.
