"Big trades" = the trades that carry the book, not big prints on the tape. The question: is there anything in the order flow — CVD, absorption, exhaustion, big prints — that marks a 3R runner apart from a scratch, at or before the fill?

Entry-time separation · BIG vs small winners

Net delta, volume, absorption, exhaustion — all dead at entry. Big-lot participation is the lone exception.

Every order-flow scalar the prior CVD study leaned on sits on the noise line. Net CVD 0.47, total volume 0.48, run-up 0.42, seller-exhaustion 0.46, absorption-at-the-low 0.53. Only big-lot participation — the fraction of the last 60s of tape volume printed in ≥10-lot orders — separates, at 0.66. And it is a magnitude signal, not a direction one: it barely moves win-vs-lose (0.54), because it can't tell you whether a bounce works, only how far it runs if it does.

The one live signal

3R runners fire into tape that is unusually thick with institutional-size prints — regardless of side.

Big-lot participation entering the trade · last 60s before fill
Bar spans the 25th–75th percentile; tick is the median. Share of window volume in ≥10-lot prints.

Why it's credible

Composition, not busyness. Total volume doesn't separate (AUC 0.48) — only the big-lot share does. Thicker tape isn't the tell; who is on it is.

Robust across time. Split-half AUC 0.65 / 0.68 — unlike the prior big-win signature, which was second-half-only.

Intraday, not a day-picker. 28 BIG trades span 24 sessions / 16 months; on days with both, the 3R trade out-participated its same-day siblings 64% of the time (0.042 vs 0.020).

What it's worth

≈2× the average R and net-per-trade, ≈3× the 3R-hit rate. A selection / sizing lever — press these bounces — not a win-rate filter.

Why absorption & exhaustion came up empty

There's no fight at the low to measure — the winners skip it.

Median depth below entry before price turns · first 5 min of the hold
How far the trade dug in against the fill. NQ ticks (0.25 pt). Lower = went straight up.

Absorption (heavy selling a price refuses to break) and exhaustion (that selling drying up) both assume a struggle at a low. But BIG trades barely make a low — 24.6 ticks (≈6 pts) — while stops dig 191. So absorption-at-the-low is if anything a loser descriptor: the stops are where sellers get absorbed — unsuccessfully — into a deeper hole. Seller-exhaustion ratio is flat across every cohort (1.6–1.8). The classic footprint read has no purchase here, and the data ceiling is real: prints carry an aggressor tag but no bid/ask-separated book, so true footprint imbalance can't be computed — only signed delta.

Phase 2 · the size-up A/B

Shipped as an engine knob and re-run through the sim. It failed. Seventh confirmation of the iron law.

The descriptor said: press the bounces that ignite where size is already trading. So the engine got a size_up_participation knob — size the entry up when the trailing big-lot share clears a threshold — and the config was swept and re-run with full re-arm (control re-run byte-identical to baseline; the knob is inert when off). The clean additive arms keep the base size and its daily-stop cadence, and only add contracts to the qualifying fills:

Net rises — but only because there are more contracts on the table (+13%). Per contract it's flat: $104.7 vs $102.6, a 2% edge inside this strategy's noise floor, bought with 31% more drawdown and a lower Sharpe. Size harder (3→6) and per-contract return drops below baseline. And the 2% doesn't even survive forward: out-of-sample (last 4 months) the size-up arms earn $34–37 per contract vs the baseline's $41 — the in-sample edge reverses.

Why the 2×-expectancy bucket evaporated: in the live engine the sized trades still win only 67% — the same rate as everything — so sizing them up just amplifies variance. The 25 sized full-stops book −$96k and drive the drawdown, and re-arm shifts which trades fire, so the bucket's realized R came in at 0.22, not the offline 0.26. A robust descriptor is not a live rule.

Verdict

Real signal, dead rule. Leave size_up_participation = 0.

Big-lot participation is the most robust entry-time separator found in either order-flow study — a genuine composition signal that clears split-half and within-session controls. But sized into the engine it adds only leverage, not allocation skill: flat per-contract in-sample, worse out-of-sample, more drawdown. It joins the nine tick overlays, the panic exit, the underwater stop and the rest — the seventh straight mined edge that does not survive as an engine rule.

The knob stays in the engine, off, for future experiments. Absorption and exhaustion were empty; big-lot participation was real but unbankable. The strategy's edge remains what the loss studies found it to be — a regime expression you ride uniformly, not a tape you can size on.
