Hunting A+ setups: the confluence is the re-entry

Continuation of the big-trade order-flow study. The tape gave one signal (big-lot participation); this pass screened 83 non-tape context features — day geometry, VWAP/profile distances, overnight & prior-day levels, regime checkpoints, trade sequence — against the 28 A+ trades (r ≥ 2) that carry 131% of the run's net.

One confluence survives everything: re-entry after a same-session stop. 34 trades, 23.5% A+ rate vs 7.0% base, avgR +0.46 vs +0.15, permutation p = 0.0008, consistent in both date halves and against a within-session control.

Its mirror is just as useful: trades taken after a scratch or a win (52% of all trades) are dead money — avgR ≈ 0, 3% A+ rate. The day's whole edge is the first attempt plus the post-stop re-entry.

And one veto: session CVD ≤ 0 at entry → 1 A+ in 68 trades (1.5%).

Engine A/B (added after the study): the finding converts. reenter_after_stop_only (v10, ghost-watch re-arm) beats the baseline on net and every risk metric — $124.5k on 222 trades vs $122.5k on 398, PF 1.98 vs 1.46, net/contract +82%, and 2× the baseline's net in the last 120 days. First knob in the series to pass. Details in §6.

1What was screened

Per-trade features computed strictly as-of entry: time-of-day and trade sequence, day geometry (run from open, HOD pullback, position in range), developing VWAP/POC/value-area distances (normalized by band width), overnight range/travel and gap, prior-day high/close/POC distances, relative volume pace, and all regime-v7 checkpoint features joined at the last checkpoint before entry. All are rank-independent of the tape signal (|ρ| ≤ 0.08 vs bigpart60), so anything that works is additive.

Verification the signal survived:

  • Permutation test: A+-rate lift p = 0.0008; avgR lift p = 0.039.
  • Definition-insensitive: "prev r < 0", "prev r ≤ −0.5", "prev r ≤ −0.9", and "prev exit == stop" all converge on the same 34 trades.
  • Not a day-type effect: on the same 27 sessions, the other trades run 7.5% A+ — base rate. The lift belongs to the re-entry, not the day.
  • Split-half: A+ rate 26.7% (H1) / 21.1% (H2). Positive net in 5 of 6 quarters; the soft spot is 2026Q2 (avgR ≈ 0 on 11 trades).

Mechanism. 85% of re-entries fill higher than the stopped entry (median +11.5 pts, median wait 23 min): the first touch gets swept, and the reclaim is the real acceptance. When the re-entry is higher, avgR = +0.57; on the 15% that re-enter lower, avgR = −0.18. Fast re-entries are the cream — wait ≤ 15 min: avgR +0.83, 38% A+ (n = 13). This independently corroborates the loser study's "59% of stops regain entry ≤ 15 min".

5The veto and the decayed tier

  • Session CVD ≤ 0 at entry (veto): 1 of 68 trades reached A+ (1.5%); 27 of 28 A+ trades entered with positive session delta. Weak as a ranker (AUC 0.62) but strong as a one-sided filter: A+ almost never happens against the session's delta.
  • RTH trend confirmation (10:30+ checkpoints): abr ≥ 0.85 / net_conviction ≥ 0.85 lift A+ to ~23% when stacked with tape (n = 30–34, avgR +0.65 to +0.73) — but the second date-half fades (31% → 14%). Watch, don't act.
  • Quiet overnight (compressed Globex): whole family points the same way (low on_abr, low band-cross, flat ON slope, small travel — AUC 0.35–0.41 inverted) but H2 decays to base. Same failure shape as previous day-level signals.
  • Dead: relative volume pace, day-of-week, gap size, prior-day high/close/POC distances, VWAP/POC/value-area geometry at entry, acceptance latency, time-of-day (mild at best). Entry geometry is remarkably uninformative — consistent with "losses are regime, not geometry" from the loss study.

6Engine A/B: the first knob in the series to pass

The naive version fails structurally. A plain halt ("any non-stop exit ends the day") produced 158 trades / $85.9k — it is exactly the first-trade-of-day cohort, and it can never take a re-entry: at 3 contracts one full stop (≈ −$2,287) already trips the $1,995 daily loss stop, so every re-armable stop in the baseline was cushioned by an earlier win or scratch. Halt that earlier trade and the re-arming stop never happens.

The shipped version watches instead of blinding. reenter_after_stop_only (engine v10): after any non-stop exit the session stands down, but would-be entries ride the full exit rules as reentry_halt ghosts in the vetoed rows — and a ghost that stops out lifts the halt. Live-tradeable: the setup fired without you and failed; the next acceptance is the re-entry. Control at v10 with the knob off is byte-identical to 30badf94.

True validation is forward data.

  • Re-arm window: rejected. Net falls monotonically as the window tightens ($124.5k → $108.6k) with no drawdown payoff — w15 even worsens maxDD. The study's "≤ 15 min is the cream" meant the fast re-entries are the best trades, not that the slow ones are bad: cutting them just costs money. Leave reentry_rearm_window_min = 0.
  • Daily loss stop: load-bearing, third confirmation. Loosening it to unlock "first-trade stop → re-entry" re-opens the bleed: worse net, much worse DD. (Both loose arms show bigger last-120d nets — a regime tease, not worth the full-window risk.) Keep $1,995.
  • Sibling config bounds the claim. On 9b7f54c6 (same family, different parameterization, window ending Feb 2026) the knob improves every risk metric (PF 2.07, DD −$11.3k, Sharpe 2.86, net/tr +58%) but costs 11% net. Decomposing the reference run by year explains it: the knob costs ≈ $23k of net in 2025 and adds ≈ $25k in 2026 — its absolute-net win lives where the baseline bleeds, which is the recent regime. The guaranteed part of the edge is efficiency (half the trades, half the commission, ~2× expectancy per trade, smaller DD); the net part is regime-dependent.
