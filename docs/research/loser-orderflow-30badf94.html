A · Entry time

You cannot smell a stop at the fill — reproduced a third time.

Best STOP-vs-everything separator over 40+ pre-entry features is pre60_runup at AUC 0.582: stops enter after a slightly bigger 60-second run-up into the fill (median 0.75 pt vs 0.25 pt) in a slightly wider range — a mild chasing signature, not tradeable at this size. Every big-print feature is ≤0.54. Stops even fill into marginally less pre-entry selling than winners (median 60s CVD −48 vs −67). Big-lot participation — the winners-study signal — is 0.47 here: it marks magnitude, not direction, exactly as Part 1 concluded.

B · Underwater — the early-warning test

At −0.40R, 116 trades died and 90 recovered to +0.55R avg. The tape can't tell them apart.

Each trade that touched −0.25R / −0.40R / −0.70R was snapshotted on the 60 seconds into that touch — live-decidable information only: volume, CVD, big-print composition, absorption (sell lots per tick of downward progress), seller exhaustion (sell rate last 20 s vs prior 40 s), and CVD divergence vs the hold low.

Every feature sits inside the noise band. Dots = AUC of stop-vs-recover at each underwater anchor (60 s tape into the touch). The one benchmark outside the band is Part 1's winners signal (pre-entry big-lot participation, BIG vs SMALLWIN, 0.664) — shown for scale. Signs also flip across anchors (seller exhaustion is "bullish" at −0.25R, "bearish" at −0.70R): classic noise. Split-half on the −0.40R top features confirms — nothing holds (0.51–0.58).

The two honest scraps, both too small to act on: stops reach −0.40R faster (median 36 s vs 50 s after entry), and recoverers show a hair more bullish CVD divergence at the touch — but at AUC 0.46/0.44 with unstable halves, this is the same graveyard every mined in-trade signal ends up in. It squares with the panic-exit A/B failure: there is no order-flow tell that a pullback is about to become a stop.

C · The stop print and its aftermath

Stops die of drift, not capitulation — and price whips violently around the corpse.

The final 60 seconds before the stop look like the rest of the hold: volume rate 1.02× the hold average, big-lot share 2.0% vs 2.1%. No flush, no climax. Selling is still accelerating when the stop fires (exhaustion ratio 1.51 — vs 0.88 at BIG winners' trail exits, where selling has dried up) and absorption is at its lowest (9.0 sell-lots per downtick vs 46 at BIG exits): price is falling easily, and the engine is right to be gone. Post-stop tape is flat (60 s CVD +9.5, big-print delta −3.5) — no institutional buyer is eating our stops.

Median post-stop path, in R of initial risk. Blue = max bounce above the exit print, red = max further fall below it, tick = where price sits at window end. Both sides grow together — 26% of stops do both (>0.5R each way) within 5 minutes. It's churn, not a clean V.

The headline temptation: within 15 minutes the median stop has bounced +1.23R, 83% bounce >0.5R, only 9% never bounce 0.25R, and 59% trade back through the full entry price (median 206 s). But the same window shows a median further fall of +0.73R and 63% falling another 0.5R first — a wider stop buys the bounce only by eating that extra drawdown, and the end-of-window distribution is wide (25th pct −0.36R, median +0.51R, 75th +1.41R). This is a SimConfig A/B question (stop_ticks wider, or re-entry after stop with re-arm), not a conclusion.

One descriptive twist that fits Part 1's magnitude story: when the final 60 s of a stop-run does carry heavy big-lot share, the flush is more terminal — post-stop bounce +0.85R vs +0.58R, further fall 0.44R vs 0.61R. Big prints end moves; they don't warn of them.

The entry-anchored features that do light up (depth-to-low 0.85, sell-volume-to-low 0.80, low-absorption 0.69) are mechanical: a stop by definition travels ~190 ticks down through sellers. They describe the label, they don't predict it.

Verdict

Losers carry no order-flow signature. Not at entry (best AUC 0.582, a mild chase tell), not underwater at matched depth (everything 0.43–0.59, unstable halves), not at the stop (1.02× ordinary tape). This is the mirror-image confirmation of Part 1: big prints size the winners; nothing on the tape flags the losers. Losses remain a regime cost, exactly as the CVD study on the sibling run concluded.

What survives as a lead: stops are frequently near a local low — 59% regain entry within 15 min, median bounce +1.23R — but the path there whips 0.73R further down first. If pursued, it's an engine A/B on stop_ticks / post-stop re-entry with re-arm, never trade-math on this table. Given that every early-exit knob A/B'd so far has lost money to the same asymmetry (the engine's patience is the edge), the prior is that wider stops trade one whip for another.
