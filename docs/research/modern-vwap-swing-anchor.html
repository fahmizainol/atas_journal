The swing anchor

Everything else in Modern VWAP [GBB] is an ordinary anchored VWAP. The one construct that isn't is the anchor itself: instead of resetting at a clock time, the accumulator resets at every confirmed swing pivot. This page walks that mechanism one bar at a time on real tape — detection, the confirmation lag, the backfill, and the step it puts in the line.

5 · Five random sessions, whole session

The same indicator laid over five randomly drawn cached sessions (seeded draw from 598 eligible days), 18:00 prior to 16:00 ET, no scrubbing — the finished line as it would have stood at the bell. It follows the knobs in section 1: switch anchor mode or the pivot/re-arm setting up there and these redraw.

6 · Two random weeks, developing weekly POC

The same construct one horizon up: the profile accumulates from the week's first tick (Sunday 18:00) and never resets, so the POC being touched is the week's consensus price. Two randomly drawn full weeks (seeded, from 102 candidate weeks with all five days cached on one contract), 10-minute bars, Sunday 18:00 through Friday 16:00. Same knobs as section 1 — and mind two things reading it: every window now counts 10-minute bars (pivot 10 = 100 minutes of confirmation), and the re-arm distance is still denominated in ticks, which is a session-scale number — against a weekly level's wander it is nearly no debounce at all. That mismatch is a finding, not a bug.

Dotted dividers are each trading day's 18:00 open. A weekly POC migrates when a new price shelf out-accumulates the old one — days later, and often hundreds of points away — so a migration here teleports the level across the whole week's range, and any touch it manufactures anchors the VWAP to a price the market chose days ago.

Reading it

  • Windows count bars, not minutes. pivot = 10 is 20 minutes of confirmation on these 2-minute bars and 10 minutes on 1-minute bars. That is a property of the indicator, not of this port — the same script on a different timeframe is a different indicator.
  • The line steps; it does not repaint. The backfill only runs forward from the confirmation bar. A backtest that re-anchors the whole history at the pivot bar is reading a line no live trader ever saw, and it will look far better than this one.
  • The last pl bars are always provisional. A pivot may be forming inside them right now. The anchor you are trading off is the one that survived, which is not the same claim as the one that was correct.
  • The POC-touch mode is an idea under evaluation, not part of the published indicator. It re-anchors where price revisits the developing point of control — "average price since the market last agreed on value". The POC here is real: accumulated tick by tick from the globex open, read at each bar close, binned to the tick. Watch two things: touches arrive in clusters (price sits at value, so the anchor shreds without a re-arm rule), and a POC migration can land the level onto price and manufacture a touch out of nothing — set re-arm to 0 and scrub to ~11:30 to see both. The re-arm distance, not the anchor, is the model.
  • Status in our work. The swing anchor is the only construct in this indicator we have neither built nor falsified elsewhere — see modern-vwap for the full study. Two things already resolved against it: the MR signal is our touch-bar close-back shape verbatim (an artifact of scoring the touch bar's own close, per the weekly-band context study), and the "two-axis regime" gate is arithmetically just KER > median — the ATR axis changes a pill's colour and gates nothing.
