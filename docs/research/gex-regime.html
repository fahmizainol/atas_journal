What actually moves the number

Holding open interest and spot fixed and advancing only the clock. Six hours of decay moves the flip by a fraction of a percent — spot dominates by roughly 10×. The time slider exists to show that, not because it is load-bearing. The last row is the least trustworthy in the table: it is the most sensitive to the arbitrary time floor used for 0DTE, where at-the-money gamma runs away as T → 0.

Notes & traps

  • Stale expiries. The pre-open snapshot still carries yesterday's expired contracts with stale gamma attached. Summing them naively overstated net GEX by 36% the first time round. Everything here is filtered to DTE >= 0.
  • Open interest is frozen intraday. OCC publishes it once, pre-open. So the entire curve is knowable at 08:00 and the live badge is an array lookup, not a Black‑Scholes sweep — which is why this needs no live options feed, only live spot.
  • Sticky strike. Each contract's IV is held fixed as spot moves. In reality downside IV rises, so the true negative-gamma region differs somewhat from the one drawn here.
  • Recompute vs feed. Black‑Scholes here (r=0) against Cboe's own published greeks at spot — the gap is shown in the cards. Fine for a binary regime read; not fine for trading wall levels precisely.
  • OI is frozen in the data, not in the market. Positions are opened all day; you just cannot see them until tomorrow morning. The curve drifts from truth as the session ages.
  • NQ price space. Levels are native NDX / QQQ. NQ futures trade at NDX plus a carry basis — rescale by the live ratio before drawing these on an NQ chart.
