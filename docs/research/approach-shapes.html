What this does and doesn't prove

The staircase band won 64% of the time across 13 separate days, so the shape is real rather than one lucky session — the best single day is only 25% of its total. But the money sits in the tail: the median staircase trade made $25, and three trades supply 44% of the +$7,960. The shape earns the win rate; only holding the winners turns that into the total. That is the same conclusion the recordings reached from the other direction — the trailed stop, not the entry, is what banked the pre-era book.

Post-era counts are thinner everywhere (n=108 against 200), and the post era traded a quieter tape — median 30-second ruler 78.5t → 62.5t. Some of the shrinkage below is the market, not the trader. The losses are not: a quieter tape cannot manufacture bigger losers.

Telling them apart at the fifteen-minute mark

Is the range wide — roughly 130 points on NQ — and has price pushed and pulled back five to nine times while still finishing nearer one extreme than the middle? That is the staircase. Ten-plus pushes finishing mid-range is the box. Barely any pullback at all is the ramp, and the ramp never paid in either era.
